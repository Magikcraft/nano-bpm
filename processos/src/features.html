The framing

A craft you fly with a droid Vision

Process OS isn’t an autopilot you hand work to. It’s a cybernetic system — you in the cockpit, an LLM as your droid copilot, and the Nano engine as the airframe — and the loop is steered, not autonomous.

The closed loop below is the instrumentation: it observes, proposes, and proves. But you fly the craft. The droid handles load, suggests variants, and reads the instruments with you; you set intent, judge what “better” means, and decide what reaches production. Self-optimization here is human-steered joint cognition.

The first target is always itself. Every Process OS ships with an empty cockpit and an empty droid slot, and individuates through use — so operating it and improving it become the same activity. You learn to fly it by tuning the very craft you’re flying.

The pilot — you

Set intent, define what “better” means this week, and hold the final word on what reaches production.

The droid — an LLM

A co-present copilot that proposes variants, repairs them against the verifier, and reasons with you — not a task fired into the cloud.

The craft — Nano + Process OS

A plastic airframe whose surfaces you reshape: the process that drives it, the prompts that drive the droid, and the experience it accrues.

You steer by reshaping three plastic surfaces — the BPM model that drives Process OS itself, the system prompts that shape the droid, and the experience it accumulates. A user task sits in that driving model, so the loop runs as a cockpit conversation rather than a fire-and-forget job — and where you place that task is the dial that hands more to the droid as the two of you grow fluent.

Flagship capability

The cockpit: investigate with a droid Live

You don’t hand Process OS a job and wait for a verdict. You open a chat over a bound trace dataset and reason with a droid that queries production itself, forms hypotheses, and proves them on the engine — a multi-turn conversation that remembers what it found.

Each workspace process binds to a trace corpus. The droid investigates it directly with a small, disciplined toolset: read-only SQL over a DuckDB view of the traces, an optional sandboxed Python tier for heavier analysis, discover_flow to recover the as-run topology, and read_model / analyze_model to inspect the BPMN that drives the process. It narrates its reasoning and every tool call as it goes, so you can follow — and challenge — how it reached a conclusion.

When it proposes a fix, the droid doesn’t hand you raw XML. It patches the model with structured edits — set a task’s job type, insert a service task, add an error-boundary event, reroute a flow, set a guard condition, add an exclusive gateway — then validate_models the result against the production parser before it ever simulates. Authored variants come back as human-readable, horizontally laid-out diagrams with named gateways and labelled conditions that you can open and download.

Supervises local llama.cpp servers from the cockpit: download models in the background, start/stop them with live status lights, ports auto-assigned, up to two at once. Model-agnostic and OpenAI-compatible — nothing has to leave your machine.

Pair AI review

A second model — Skeptic, Synthesizer, or Refiner — critiques the primary droid’s answer in its own bubble, reasoning and all, so a cross-check is built into the loop rather than bolted on after.

Steerable, resumable sessions

Carry one model’s conclusion into the next, switch the answering model mid-investigation, steer or wrap up a turn, and reattach to an in-flight investigation after a reload — each bubble keeps the model that produced it.

Ask a model for better ways to run a process — then prove every suggestion on the real engine before trusting it. The LLM proposes; the engine decides.

Process OS folds production traces into a performance report (objective, per-task worker catalogue with cost / latency / quality, measured baseline) and hands that report — not a firehose of raw events — to a reasoning model. The model returns candidate variants: which worker should run each task, and optionally a restructured BPMN model.

Each candidate is then speculatively executed: every input is replayed through a fresh instance of the real engine-core — the same engine production runs — on a virtual clock, collecting end-to-end latency, cost, incidents, and correctness. A hallucinated “improvement” that does not actually measure better is caught by the numbers and ranked below the baseline; structurally invalid proposals are rejected with a reason.

◇ The Nano Alternate Reality Engine

Every hypothesis gets its own universe. Process OS forks a multiverse of “what-ifs?” — each candidate variant runs in a fully isolated alternate reality, replaying the same production inputs through its own fresh engine-core on a private virtual clock.

Just like speculative execution in a CPU, Nano explores many branches of how the process could run at once — different worker assignments, restructured models, alternate routings — and lets reality, not rhetoric, decide which branch to keep. Realities that measure worse than today collapse; the one that genuinely moves the objective is promoted. Because every engine is cheap, deterministic, and WASM-portable, the multiverse is effectively free to spin up and explore at scale.

Many realities run. One survives contact with the numbers.

  • Model-agnostic and OpenAI-compatible — points at a local llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM, or a hosted provider; keys and egress stay inside the optimization boundary.
  • Every candidate is feasibility-gated on correctness and incident rate, then ordered by the scenario objective (cost, latency, or incidents).
  • Rejected proposals are reported, not hidden — you see what the model tried and why it was discarded.

Flagship capability

Cohort Deployment to Production In design

A proven variant earns a slice of real traffic before it earns all of it. Promotion is gradual, measured, and reversible.

A winning hypothesis is not flipped on globally. Process OS deploys it to a cohort — a bounded fraction of live instances routed to the new variant — while the rest continue on the incumbent. Both cohorts are measured against the same objective from the same live trace contract, so the comparison is apples-to-apples on production load rather than on a simulator.

If the cohort holds its gains and its gates, its share grows; if it regresses, it is rolled back automatically. This is the actuation half of the loop and the one place the engine exposes a control surface to the optimizer — a deliberate, auditable routing primitive rather than ad-hoc rewiring.

  • Bounded blast radius: a bad variant can only ever affect its cohort.
  • Live A/B on the objective, not a synthetic proxy — the production numbers decide whether the rollout advances.
  • Reversible by construction: every promotion step has a defined rollback.

Status: the routing/actuation primitive is specified in the design series and is the next keystone on the roadmap; today the loop runs up to ranked, gate-passing recommendations.

The substrate underneath

The two flagship capabilities stand on a small set of disciplined primitives.

Insights from the trace contract

Reads Nano over its public trace / metrics export and folds it into a per-process, per-element report: durations, queue vs. service time, bottlenecks, and incident clusters. GET /api/insights.

Real-engine simulation

The SimRunner drives the actual engine-core on a virtual clock, so measured latency, cost, and correctness transfer to production rather than modelling an approximation of it.

Live cluster measurement

The ClusterRunner acts and measures against a running multi-node cluster, unioning partition-local traces cluster-wide and sensing per-node backlog.

Queueing-model staffing

An Erlang-C M/M/c model fitted from measured per-job-type arrival and service times recommends how many workers hold a target p99 queue-wait. GET /api/harness/cluster?targetP99Ms=.

Structured BPMN authoring & validation

The droid edits the process model through verb-level operations and a round-trippable engine model — never hand-rolled XML — and every candidate is parsed by the production parser, with dangling references and structural faults reported and fixed before simulation.

Camunda 8 import

Fold a Camunda 8 / Zeebe record export (NDJSON, JSON array, or Elasticsearch _source) into a Nano trace corpus — including tier-2 job stimuli for faithful replay — so an existing C8 process can be optimized in the cockpit. processos import-camunda <input> <out-dir>.

One-way dependency

Process OS runs as its own process and only ever reads Nano over HTTP. Nano never links or knows about it — run production without it and nothing changes.

Advisory by default

Everything short of cohort deployment is a recommendation. The engine and the measured numbers are authoritative; the optimizer never silently rewires a running system.

The governing principle: the LLM may propose anything, but nothing reaches production unproven. Simulation measures, gates feasibility, and cohorts contain the risk — the engine, not the model, has the final word.
